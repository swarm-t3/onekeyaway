Is your protocol one stolen key away from a drain?

In the first eight months of 2026, DeFi lost at least $1.3B to hacks, and leaked private keys and admin access overtook smart contract bugs as the top cause (source). Wasabi Protocol lost about $4.5M in April because one deployer EOA could upgrade everything (CoinDesk, Halborn).

An audit checks the code. It doesn't tell you who holds the keys. OneKeyAway does that: for any contract it reads the proxy admin, the beacon and owner(), and it follows ProxyAdmins and Safes down to the keys that actually sign.

What we found

Contracts that hold TVL: we read the control path of about 2,000 contracts from about 480 protocols, using addresses from DefiLlama's open-source TVL adapters. Each hit was then checked by hand, and we dropped third-party tokens and factories that can't touch existing funds. At least 35 protocols and token issuers have a core or fund-holding contract whose upgrade path ends at one EOA, with no on-chain multisig and no timelock.
  • Recently launched DeFi: 10 of the 125 protocols listed in the last 12 months, including a ~$157M stablecoin wrapper, a ~$142M BTC yield vault and a perps bridge that holds user deposits.
  • BTC and stablecoin yield: several vault and receipt tokens backing $100M-$300M protocols.
  • Tokenised assets: the largest issuers do it too. USDC, PYUSD, USDtb, BUIDL-I and several tokenised fund shares are upgradeable by a single EOA. These issuers almost certainly keep that key in an HSM, which is the point: the safety lives off-chain, where nobody else can see it, and there is no timelock that would give holders warning.

Governance and protocol tokens (480 scanned)

We took every DeFi protocol on DefiLlama with at least $300k TVL that lists an EVM governance or protocol token on one of 15 chains, then read each token's control path on-chain. Scanned 2026-10-06. These are public on-chain facts, and each address links to a block explorer so you can check it. "Single key" here means one EOA, which may be MPC- or HSM-backed off-chain. A single-EOA owner is not automatically dangerous: what matters is what that owner can call. For example, the Superform UP token has an EOA owner, but its mint is locked for 3 years and capped at 2% a year. Upgrade rights and mint rights are the serious cases.
